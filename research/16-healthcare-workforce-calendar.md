# 16 — Healthcare workforce planning and staffing: the recurring task calendar

This chapter maps every recurring administrative, compliance and operational task in US healthcare workforce planning and staffing — on the staffing-agency side (cluster J, [ideas 61–65](12-healthcare-staffing.md)) and the facility/health-system side (cluster L, [ideas 72–77](15-healthcare-workforce-planning.md)) — organized by how often the task recurs, the way [08-regulatory-calendar.md](08-regulatory-calendar.md) organizes the same kind of material by date. For every task we give the regulation or industry requirement and its source, who must do it and roughly how many US organizations that means, the consequence of missing it, and an explicit call on whether one of the twelve existing ideas already covers it or it is a genuine gap. Method matches every other chapter: primary sources first (CMS, OIG, IRS, HHS OCR, Joint Commission, NCQA, state statutes, named trade associations), a labelled industry estimate when no regulator publishes a number, and "not found" rather than an invented citation — several rows below carry that label rather than a guessed penalty amount or headcount.

## Weekly

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nurse schedule publication and shift-swap/self-scheduling cycle | No federal cadence rule; state ratio laws require the published schedule to satisfy required ratios every shift — California Title 22 §70217/AB 394, Oregon HB 2697, Massachusetts 958 CMR 8.00 (sources in [15-healthcare-workforce-planning.md](15-healthcare-workforce-planning.md#sources)) | Facility — scheduling managers at ~5,121 AHA community hospitals and ~14,700–15,400 CMS-certified SNFs (AHA 2026; KFF/Definitive Healthcare) | A published schedule that violates a ratio law draws per-violation fines (California SB 227: $15,000 first/$30,000 subsequent; Oregon: up to $5,000) | Covered — [idea 74](15-healthcare-workforce-planning.md#74-ai-schedule-optimization-and-coverage-forecasting-for-small-to-mid-hospitals-and-nursing-homes) (schedule generator, compliance checker, self-scheduling portal) |
| Timesheet approval and agency-invoice reconciliation for per-diem and travel staff | FLSA wage-payment timing plus state wage-payment-frequency statutes (most states require weekly, biweekly or semimonthly pay for non-exempt workers; e.g. New York Labor Law §191 requires manual workers paid weekly) | Agency — ~2,927 healthcare staffing firms (IBISWorld) running weekly/biweekly per-diem and travel payroll; Facility — back offices reconciling agency invoices against signed timesheets | Late or inaccurate pay triggers state wage-payment penalties (liquidated damages in several states); facilities that skip reconciliation overpay for hours never worked | Covered — [idea 62](12-healthcare-staffing.md#62-pay-package-builder-stipend-compliance-and-facility-invoicing-for-small-travel-and-per-diem-nurse-agencies) (timesheet capture → invoice generator) on the agency side, [idea 64](12-healthcare-staffing.md#64-facility-side-agency-vendor-management-lite-for-independent-snfs-rural-hospitals-and-home-care) (invoice-vs-timesheet audit) on the facility side |
| Real-time/per-shift staffing-ratio posting | California Title 22 §70217 requires the "current, actual" unit staffing plan to be posted and available at all times; SB 596 allows counting each day of noncompliance separately | Facility — California's general acute-care hospitals bound by the state ratio law (not found: an exact current count of California hospitals in scope) | A cited posting or ratio gap becomes its own stackable CDPH fine under SB 596 | Covered — [idea 74](15-healthcare-workforce-planning.md#74-ai-schedule-optimization-and-coverage-forecasting-for-small-to-mid-hospitals-and-nursing-homes) |
| Fair Workweek/predictive-scheduling advance notice and predictability pay | Most US predictive-scheduling ordinances (Oregon statewide; NYC, Seattle, Los Angeles, Philadelphia, San Francisco, Berkeley, Emeryville, Evanston) exclude healthcare employers; Chicago's Fair Workweek Ordinance is the one jurisdiction that reaches healthcare | Facility — healthcare employers physically inside Chicago city limits only, a small fraction of the ~5,121 community hospitals nationally | A schedule change inside the 14-day notice window without a qualifying exception triggers predictability pay (typically one extra hour, more for a cancelled or shortened shift) | GAP — no idea models Chicago's predictability-pay rule specifically, but the addressable population (one city) is too narrow to justify a dedicated build; see Gap analysis |

## Monthly

| Task | Requirement & source | Who (agency/facility) & how many | Consequence of missing it | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OIG-LEIE and SAM.gov exclusion screening | No statute names an exact frequency for providers themselves, but 42 CFR 455.436(c)(2) binds state Medicaid agencies and MCOs to check "no less frequently than monthly," and OIG's own compliance guidance is the basis every credentialing vendor and Joint Commission HCSS reviewer treats as the de facto employer standard (cms.gov toolkit for 42 CFR 455.436; ecfr.gov) | Agency — ~2,927 healthcare staffing firms (IBISWorld) screening every placed clinician; Facility — every hospital (6,093, AHA), SNF (~14,700–15,400) and the 6,000+ NAMSS-member medical staff offices screening employed, agency and locum practitioners alike | Billing for an excluded individual's services risks a civil monetary penalty of roughly $25,595 per item or service claimed under the 2026 inflation-adjusted table (figure per a compliance vendor's summary — accountablehq.com — the primary eCFR/Federal Register page blocked automated retrieval in this pass, so treat the exact dollar amount as sourced-but-unverified against the government text), plus treble-damages assessments and False Claims Act exposure | Covered — [idea 61](12-healthcare-staffing.md#61-credentialing-and-multi-state-licence-tracker-for-small-healthcare-staffing-agencies) (agency roster) and [idea 73](15-healthcare-workforce-planning.md#73-facility-side-credentialing-and-compliance-oversight-for-one-hospitals-medical-staff-office) (facility-wide roster) |
| State Medicaid exclusion-list screening beyond the federal LEIE/SAM | 44 states maintain their own Medicaid exclusion list separate from the federal LEIE; monthly screening is already mandatory by state rule in at least 14 of them, and roughly half of state exclusions never appear on the federal list at all (ethico.com; paulweidenfeld.com; exclusionscreening.com) | Agency and facility — same populations as the row above | Same CMP/False Claims exposure as federal exclusions — screening LEIE/SAM alone misses real excluded individuals in the states with their own list | Partial — idea 61's and idea 73's published MVP scope names "LEIE and SAM" matching only, not the 44 state lists. A feature-completeness gap inside an already-covered idea, not a new product: dedicated vendors (ProviderTrust, Ethico, Exclusion Screening LLC) already sell this exact feed to the broader healthcare-compliance market |
| Travel/per-diem stipend "tax home" documentation | IRS Publication 463's tax-home test and the accountable-plan rules under IRC §62(c)/Treas. Reg. §1.62-2 require contemporaneous evidence that an assignment is temporary and that stipends don't exceed the GSA per-diem benchmark (irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p463.pdf) | Agency — travel/per-diem-heavy firms among the ~2,927; ultimate audit risk sits with the clinician, but the agency's plan design is what shifts exposure onto the whole roster | An IRS reclassification of stipends as wages creates back payroll tax and penalty exposure across every clinician paid under the same plan design, not just one return | Partial — [idea 62](12-healthcare-staffing.md#62-pay-package-builder-stipend-compliance-and-facility-invoicing-for-small-travel-and-per-diem-nurse-agencies) checks each package against the GSA benchmark and runs a one-time tax-home eligibility questionnaire, but its MVP does not retain the ongoing documentary evidence (lease, mortgage, utility proof) an audit would request — that record-keeping duty sits with the clinician today (directional industry sources: advantismed.com, betternurse.org, not a regulator). Too thin alone for a new product; a plausible feature add to 62 |
| Nursys e-Notify / state licence status monitoring | NCSBN's Nursys e-Notify gives free real-time RN/LPN licence and discipline alerts to enrolled employers (nursys.com) | Agency and facility — any employer of RNs/LPNs enrolled in e-Notify | A lapsed or disciplined licence not caught in time means practicing, or continuing to place a clinician, without a valid licence | Covered — [idea 61](12-healthcare-staffing.md#61-credentialing-and-multi-state-licence-tracker-for-small-healthcare-staffing-agencies) and [idea 73](15-healthcare-workforce-planning.md#73-facility-side-credentialing-and-compliance-oversight-for-one-hospitals-medical-staff-office) |
| Continuing-education (CE) due-date monitoring | State-by-state CE requirements tied to each licence's renewal cycle; CE Broker (Propelus) tracks over 2 million nurses nationally (cebroker.com) | Agency and facility — every state-licensed clinician on either side | A lapsed CE requirement blocks licence renewal, which blocks continued placement or privileging | Covered — [idea 61](12-healthcare-staffing.md#61-credentialing-and-multi-state-licence-tracker-for-small-healthcare-staffing-agencies) (seed 50-state CE matrix) |
| ACA 130-hour-per-month full-time-equivalence tracking for PRN/per-diem staff | IRC §4980H defines full-time as averaging 30 hours/week or 130 hours/month; an Applicable Large Employer (50+ FTEs) must track this at the individual level every month, not only in aggregate (trusaic.com; points-north.com) | Agency — Applicable-Large-Employer-sized staffing firms among the ~2,927 (most firms running dozens of clinicians on assignment cross 50 FTEs) | Missing a PRN worker's crossing into full-time status shows up on Form 1095-C as a coverage-offer failure and can draw an IRS Letter 226-J proposed-payment notice | GAP relative to ideas 61–65 (idea 62 explicitly defers payroll filing to a processor partner) — but already served at the market level by staffing-aware ACA vendors (ACA-Track names "ACA compliance for staffing agencies" as its own page; Points North and Trusaic both sell variable-hour/PRN tracking). Not a defensible new-product gap |

## Quarterly

| Task | Requirement & source | Who (agency/facility) & how many | Consequence of missing it | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Payroll-Based Journal (PBJ) submission | Due 45 days after each fiscal quarter ends; the April 2026 iQIES file-specification update (v4.10.0) is now in effect (cms.gov/medicare/quality/nursing-home-improvement/staffing-data-submission) | Facility — ~14,700–15,400 CMS-certified skilled nursing facilities (KFF; Definitive Healthcare) | A missed or late submission triggers an automatic one-star Five-Star staffing rating regardless of actual staffing levels | Covered — [idea 63](12-healthcare-staffing.md#63-payroll-based-journal-pbj-submission-builder-for-skilled-nursing-facilities), from the original 65-idea cluster (`research/12-healthcare-staffing.md`), outside both idea ranges this brief targets — flagged per the research instructions rather than re-scored here |
| Joint Commission HCSS quarterly performance-measure filing | Must be filed within 45 days of each calendar quarter's end to maintain Health Care Staffing Services certification (jointcommission.org) | Agency — any of the ~2,927 firms holding or seeking HCSS certification | Missed quarterly filings put the 2-year certification cycle (see Multi-year, below) at risk | Covered — [idea 61](12-healthcare-staffing.md#61-credentialing-and-multi-state-licence-tracker-for-small-healthcare-staffing-agencies) |
| Illinois Nurse Agency Licensing Act quarterly rate report | 225 ILCS 510, amended 2022, requires licensed nurse agencies to report average rates charged and paid, by facility, county and provider type, through the state's NALA Portal each quarter (labor.illinois.gov; ogletree.com, already cited in idea 64) | Agency — Illinois-licensed nurse agencies only; not found: a public headcount of currently licensed agencies (IDOL publishes an annual compilation of the quarterly data, not a live roster) | Not found — the statute's specific penalty or licence-suspension trigger for a missed filing was not located in this pass | GAP — neither [idea 61](12-healthcare-staffing.md#61-credentialing-and-multi-state-licence-tracker-for-small-healthcare-staffing-agencies) (clinician-side credentialing) nor [idea 64](12-healthcare-staffing.md#64-facility-side-agency-vendor-management-lite-for-independent-snfs-rural-hospitals-and-home-care) (which checks *other* agencies' registration from the facility's side) files this for the agency itself. One state, narrow — a feature an idea-61-style tool could add for Illinois customers, not a standalone product |
| CAQH ProView 120-day (180-day in Illinois) re-attestation | CAQH requires every enrolled provider to log in and re-attest profile accuracy at least every 120 days or the profile is marked "Expired," freezing every payer and hospital credentialing action tied to it (hiregaynell.com, primecredential.com — directional industry sources; the exact day-count was not confirmed against a CAQH-published page in this pass) | Physicians and other CAQH-enrolled providers nationally; acutely relevant to the ~52,000–57,000 active locum physicians in idea 65's population who juggle one profile across every agency and hospital that pulls from it | An expired CAQH profile stalls every pending or future credentialing action referencing it, at every agency and facility simultaneously, at the same moment | Partial/GAP — [idea 65](12-healthcare-staffing.md#65-locum-tenens-contract-and-credential-packet-manager-for-independent-physicians) names CAQH ProView only as a competitor reference ("the free national profile physicians already maintain"), not as a tracked renewal date among its own "IMLC and state-licence status... renewal alerts." A real, dated, missable task idea 65's design should track but does not yet name — a feature to add, not a new product |

## Annual

| Task | Requirement & source | Who (agency/facility) & how many | Consequence of missing it | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TB screening, flu-vaccination attestation and other occupational-health re-verification (Hep B series, N95 respirator fit-testing, titers) | CMS Conditions of Participation require facility infection-control programs (42 CFR 482.42); OSHA's Respiratory Protection Standard (29 CFR 1910.134) requires annual N95 fit-testing for staff who use one; CDC/ACIP guidance drives near-universal facility flu-vaccination policy | Every hospital and SNF employee, plus every travel/per-diem clinician who must re-clear the same tests at each new facility — the full clinical workforce across ~5,121 community hospitals and ~14,700–15,400 SNFs, with the acute repeat-testing pain concentrated on the hundreds of thousands of travel/per-diem placements made each year across the ~2,927 staffing agencies | A facility that admits a clinician without current infection-control clearance risks a Condition-of-Participation citation on survey; a clinician who cannot produce current proof cannot start an assignment — the agency's and facility's cost in lost time, not a regulator fine | GAP relative to ideas 61–65 and 72–77 — none of the twelve builds a shared occupational-health record. But this is the one candidate in this brief with real, resourced external competitors already selling exactly this workflow — see Gap analysis |
| ACA 1095-C/1094-C reporting for the staffing agency as an Applicable Large Employer | IRC §§6055–6056; furnish Form 1095-C to employees by March 2, e-file 1094-C/1095-C with the IRS by March 31 for electronic filers (irs.gov/instructions/i109495c) | Agency — staffing firms with 50+ full-time-equivalent employees, most of the ~2,927 firms above the smallest tier | Incorrect offer codes, missed months or wrong safe-harbor elections are the leading trigger of an IRS Letter 226-J proposed-payment notice (per ACA-compliance vendors; exact per-employee penalty figures not independently re-verified against irs.gov in this pass) | GAP relative to ideas 61–65 — idea 62 explicitly defers "running payroll or filing taxes directly" to a processor partner. Not a defensible new-product gap: ACA-Track names "ACA compliance for staffing agencies" as its own page, and Points North and Trusaic both sell dedicated variable-hour/PRN ACA tracking — three funded, staffing-aware incumbents already compete here |
| W-2 and 1099-NEC issuance | IRC §6051 (W-2, due to employees and SSA by Jan 31) and §6041, with the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's 1099-NEC threshold raised to $2,000 for payments after Dec 31, 2025 (already sourced in idea 62 via onpay.com, avalara.com) | Agency — every staffing firm running W-2 clinicians, 1099 locums, or both | Late or incorrect information returns draw IRS late-filing penalties per form, escalating with delay | GAP relative to ideas 61–65 (idea 62 explicitly stops short of running payroll) — but generic to every US business and already served by every payroll processor (Gusto, ADP, QuickBooks Payroll, the same processors idea 62 already plans to export to). Not a defensible niche |
| Workers' compensation policy renewal and premium audit | State workers'-compensation statutes plus the insurer's own policy-audit clause; renewal timing follows the policy's effective date, typically annual | Agency — every staffing firm; payroll volatility (clinicians starting and ending assignments mid-policy-year, multi-state placements) makes the year-end true-up audit unusually painful for a healthcare staffing back office specifically | An audit that finds under-reported payroll produces an additional premium bill, sometimes months after the policy year closed and the cash is already spent elsewhere | GAP relative to ideas 61–65. Real pain, thin software opportunity: the closest dedicated product found, workerscompauditprep.com, is a manual spreadsheet-workbook toolkit for general SMBs and contractors, not automated and not healthcare-staffing-specific; the more structural fix already sold into staffing agencies is "pay-as-you-go" workers' comp (Asure, E-COMP, and most staffing-focused PEOs), which reports actual payroll each pay period and removes the year-end surprise by construction. A point solution competes against a substitution many agencies already adopt |
| Certificate of insurance (COI) generation and renewal per facility placement | Contractual — facility risk-management departments require a COI naming the facility as certificate holder/additional insured before placing staff; not a public regulation | Agency — effectively every one of the ~2,927 firms, reissuing or refreshing a COI each time a new facility relationship starts and again whenever the underlying malpractice/general-liability policy renews | No COI on file blocks a placement from starting, at the facility's discretion; no regulator penalty attaches to the agency | GAP relative to ideas 61–65, but thin at the market level: most insurance brokers issue a COI same-day through a self-service portal at no incremental cost, capping willingness to pay. The inbound-tracking tools that exist (TrustLayer, Certificial, myCOI) solve the opposite direction — a facility collecting COIs from many vendors — not an agency issuing its own COI outward to many facilities, and none names healthcare staffing as a specific vertical |
| State nurse/staffing-agency registration or licence renewal (the agency's own) | Named state statutes already sourced in idea 64 — Illinois (225 ILCS 510), Minnesota, Massachusetts (101 CMR 345.00), Connecticut (annual fee since Jan 1, 2023), Iowa ($500/year since Jul 1, 2022), Indiana (2023 law) | Agency — only firms operating in these roughly six to eight states | Operating unregistered, or after a lapsed registration, exposes the agency to the same risk idea 64 describes for a facility that hires an unregistered agency — but here it is the agency's own standing at risk | GAP relative to ideas 61–65 — idea 64 tracks this from the facility's side (checking whether an agency it's considering is registered), but no idea helps the agency file its own renewal. Narrow (a handful of states) and low-frequency; a plausible feature add to idea 61, not a standalone product |
| Malpractice and general/professional liability insurance policy renewal (the underlying annual policy, distinct from the per-placement COI above) | Facility contract minimums (commonly $1M/$3M for medical professional liability, per industry sources) plus, in some states, licensing-level minimums | Agency — every firm placing clinical staff | A lapsed policy blocks every active placement simultaneously, not just a delayed new one | GAP relative to ideas 61–65, but well-served already by a specialty insurance-brokerage market built around healthcare staffing specifically (CM&F Group, Insureon, MEDPLI, Mason McBride, Homewood all publish healthcare-staffing-specific pages) — a relationship-and-underwriting business, not a software gap |
| Ongoing Professional Practice Evaluation (OPPE) | Joint Commission standard requiring practice-pattern review at least every 12 months for every privileged practitioner (jointcommission.org, already sourced in idea 73) | Facility — medical staff offices credentialing any practitioner, employed, agency or locum | A facility that cannot produce current OPPE data on survey risks a finding tied to the same 2025–26 NCQA/Joint Commission tightening idea 73 already describes | Covered — [idea 73](15-healthcare-workforce-planning.md#73-facility-side-credentialing-and-compliance-oversight-for-one-hospitals-medical-staff-office) ("an OPPE/FPPE due-date tracker") |
| New York hospital clinical staffing plan — annual submission plus semiannual review | NY Public Health Law §2805-t requires each general hospital to adopt and submit a clinical staffing plan annually (first due July 1, 2022) and to conduct a semiannual review of it (law.justia.com) | Facility — New York's general hospitals (not found: a current exact count re-verified in this pass) | The statute names a missed semiannual review or annual submission as an investigable violation; a specific dollar penalty was not found in this pass | Partial — idea 74's "generic configurable-ratio mode for other states" could technically absorb this, but the chapter never names New York's law specifically, and 2805-t is a committee/disclosure regime rather than a numeric ratio — a meaningfully different rules shape. A feature idea 74 should name explicitly, not a new product |

## Multi-year (2–3+ years)

| Task | Requirement & source | Who (agency/facility) & how many | Consequence of missing it | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Joint Commission Health Care Staffing Services (HCSS) certification renewal | Certification runs on a 2-year cycle with an on-site or off-year review and continuous quarterly performance-measure filing throughout (jointcommission.org; favoritestaffing.com) | Agency — any of the ~2,927 firms seeking or holding HCSS certification, concentrated among firms selling into hospital systems that require it as a vendor condition | Lapsed certification can cost the agency hospital-system contracts that name HCSS as a requirement | Covered — [idea 61](12-healthcare-staffing.md#61-credentialing-and-multi-state-licence-tracker-for-small-healthcare-staffing-agencies) |
| NCQA/Joint Commission primary-source-verification (PSV) recredentialing cycle | NCQA's July 2025 standards update shortened the PSV window to 120 days (90 for certified CVOs) per verification event, with full recredentialing at least every 36 months; Joint Commission requires recredentialing every 2–3 years alongside annual OPPE (already sourced in idea 73) | Facility — medical staff offices credentialing every privileged practitioner regardless of employer | Failing the tightened 2025–26 standard is already causing 2026-cycle survey failures per idea 73's own sourcing | Covered — [idea 73](15-healthcare-workforce-planning.md#73-facility-side-credentialing-and-compliance-oversight-for-one-hospitals-medical-staff-office). No agency-side equivalent gap exists: a staffing agency's obligation is continuous monitoring (Nursys e-Notify, monthly LEIE/SAM) rather than a triennial PSV redo, and idea 61 already covers that continuous-monitoring model |
| National Practitioner Data Bank (NPDB) query | The Health Care Quality Improvement Act of 1986 requires a hospital to query the NPDB at initial privileging and at least every 2 years thereafter for a practitioner continuously on staff (npdb.hrsa.gov; providertrust.com) | Facility — hospitals credentialing a medical staff, a subset of AHA's 6,093 hospitals; also required at initial credentialing for agency/locum placements | Not found — the Data Bank's own enforcement mechanism for a missed query was not located in this pass; the practical consequence is an incomplete privileging file that fails a Joint Commission or NCQA record review | Partial — idea 73's write-up never names the NPDB specifically, though a real facility-credentialing product could not omit it in practice. A feature to name explicitly, not a new product |
| DEA registration renewal | DEA registrations renew on a 3-year cycle for practitioners who prescribe controlled substances (already sourced in idea 65 as a tracked field) | Agency/facility — physicians and other prescribing clinicians | A lapsed DEA registration stops a prescriber from writing controlled-substance orders, suspending part of their clinical function | Covered — [idea 65](12-healthcare-staffing.md#65-locum-tenens-contract-and-credential-packet-manager-for-independent-physicians) ("DEA... expiry tracking"), and implicitly idea 73 |
| State clinical-licence renewal where the state's cycle is biennial rather than annual | State-by-state — many boards of nursing renew every 2 years (e.g., California); others are annual; the Nursys/compact landscape described in idea 61 spans both | Agency and facility — every state-licensed clinician on either side | Practicing on a lapsed licence is practicing without one — the most basic failure a credentialing product exists to prevent | Covered — [idea 61](12-healthcare-staffing.md#61-credentialing-and-multi-state-licence-tracker-for-small-healthcare-staffing-agencies) (Nursys e-Notify, licence-expiration calendar) and idea 65/73 for physicians and privileged practitioners |
| CMS Payroll-Based Journal / Five-Star methodology overhauls | Periodic CMS-driven changes to the PBJ file specification and Five-Star staffing calculation — most recently the April 2026 iQIES cutover (v4.10.0) and new weekend-hours/turnover measures (already sourced in idea 63) | Facility — the ~14,700–15,400 CMS-certified SNFs filing PBJ every quarter | A facility whose PBJ tooling doesn't track a methodology change risks a rejected submission or a miscalculated Five-Star preview | Covered — [idea 63](12-healthcare-staffing.md#63-payroll-based-journal-pbj-submission-builder-for-skilled-nursing-facilities), outside both idea ranges this brief targets — flagged per instructions |

## Gap analysis

Mapped against a full cadence — weekly through multi-year — the twelve existing ideas cover the calendar's structural spine well. Every task with a clear regulatory driver and a fixed date already has a defensible owner: monthly exclusion screening sits with idea 61 (agency) and idea 73 (facility); quarterly PBJ and HCSS filings sit with idea 63 and idea 61; annual OPPE sits with idea 73; multi-year PSV recredentialing, HCSS certification and DEA/licence renewal sit with 73, 61 and 65 respectively. The near-misses found in this pass are almost all about *breadth*, not architecture: idea 61 and 73 both scope their exclusion-matching to "LEIE and SAM" when 44 states run their own additional Medicaid exclusion list; idea 65 names CAQH ProView as a competitor but doesn't track its 120-day re-attestation clock; idea 74's configurable ratio-law engine doesn't yet name New York's staffing-committee disclosure regime by statute. These are backlog items for a founder building 61, 65 or 73 — additional feeds and named rules inside a product that already exists — not evidence of an uncovered market.

The two candidates this brief was asked to prioritize both looked strong going in and did not survive research. **Occupational-health re-verification churn** — the real pain that a travel clinician's annual TB test, flu attestation and N95 fit-test have to be re-proven at every new facility even though the underlying medical fact is unchanged — is genuine and well-documented (myn95certificate.com's very existence as a single-purpose product is evidence of the pain). But it is already commercially solved at real scale: BlueHive is a funded, HRIS-integrated (240+ integrations), 20,000-clinic-network platform whose own marketing states that "portable digital health records travel with healthcare professionals... screenings can be shared with new facilities without repeating tests," sold to employers and staffing organizations as the buyer; Kamana — already listed as a competitor in idea 61 — offers the same portability for immunization records for free as part of its clinician "digital wallet." Building a fourth entrant into a space with two well-resourced incumbents already selling the exact workflow fails this research's own competitive-gap criterion the same way idea 76's GME-compliance candidate was dropped for New Innovations' dominance.

**An agency-side employer-compliance calendar** bundling ACA 1095-C, workers' comp audit prep and COI management also weakens on inspection. ACA 1095-C compliance for variable-hour staffing rosters is already a named vertical for three funded vendors (ACA-Track, Points North, Trusaic) — one of which markets "ACA compliance for staffing agencies" as its own product page. Workers' comp audit pain is real, but the market's actual fix is structural (pay-as-you-go premium models sold through staffing-focused PEOs) rather than better audit-prep software, and the one dedicated audit-prep product found is a manual spreadsheet toolkit, not evidence of unmet demand for automation. COI issuance turns out to be a same-day, broker-handled, largely free process — a real annoyance but not a $100+/month problem, and the COI-tracking vendors that do exist (TrustLayer, Certificial, myCOI) solve the opposite direction of the workflow (an organization collecting many vendors' COIs) rather than a staffing agency issuing its own COI outward. Bundling three individually thin or already-served tasks into one product does not manufacture a strong one.

Two smaller items are worth a founder's attention without being their own idea: Illinois's NALA quarterly rate report and the agency's own state registration renewals (Iowa, Minnesota, Massachusetts, Connecticut, Indiana) are real, sourced, unaddressed tasks — but each affects at most a handful of states and a modest filing burden, well short of a standalone product. The honest finding is that this calendar, unlike the sports and rural-hospital gaps found in earlier chapters, did its job by confirming coverage rather than by surfacing a hidden $100k-MRR idea — which is itself a useful result for a founder deciding where to spend the next several months.
